## Lesson 1 · Is it alive? The 7 life processes (Unit 4) → `lessons/l01-life-processes.html`

- MCQ 1. Living things produce young ones by ___. A) Respiration B) **Reproduction** C) Nutrition D) Excretion
- MCQ 2. What do plants need to make their own food through photosynthesis? A) **Sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide** B) Oxygen, soil, and darkness C) Meat and sugary drinks D) Plastic and rocks
- MCQ 8. Which process involves taking in food for energy and growth? A) Excretion B) **Nutrition** C) Movement D) Sensitivity
- MCQ 9. What is the term for getting rid of waste products that the body has made? A) Digestion B) **Excretion** C) Inhalation D) Absorption
- MCQ 10. Which of these living things can make its own food using sunlight? A) Mushroom B) **Oak tree** C) Earthworm D) Lion
- MCQ 11. Why can animals move around easily while plants mostly stay in one place? A) Plants do not have muscles or legs to walk. B) Plants are scared of moving. C) Animals need to find food, while plants make their own food in place. D) **Both A and C**
- MCQ 14. What does "growth" mean in biology? A) Changing clothes as you get older B) **A permanent increase in size and mass** C) Moving from one room to another D) Learning how to speak
- Q2 Complete the sentences:
  1. Another word for taking in nutrition is **feeding**.
  2. Polar bears can **sense** things in their environment. For example, with their nose they can sense the smell of meat.
  3. All living organisms **excrete** waste substances. Animals excrete **carbon dioxide** when they breathe out.
  4. Living organisms **reproduce** to make more of the same kind of organism.
  5. Young plants and animals get bigger. This is called **growth**.
  6. All living organisms break down some of the food they eat, to provide them with energy. This happens in a process called **respiration**.
  7. Most living organisms can change the shape and position of their bodies. This is called **movement**.
- Q6 Matching (draw one line from each characteristic to its description):
  nutrition → feeding: taking in materials from the environment for energy and growth ·
  respiration → breaking down nutrients to release energy ·
  excretion → getting rid of waste materials ·
  reproduction → making new individuals of the same kind of organism ·
  growth → getting bigger ·
  sensitivity → detecting changes in the environment ·
  movement → changing the position of part or all of the body
- Q7(b) Growth is one characteristic of living organisms. Write down three other characteristics shown in the pictures (zebras: one grazing, one walking, an adult with a foal). Answer key: **Reproduction, Respiration, Movement** (nutrition is also shown by the grazing zebra).

## Lesson 2 · Viruses: alive or not? (Unit 4) → `lessons/l02-viruses.html`

- MCQ 3. Why is a virus considered non-living by most scientists? A) It is too heavy. B) **It cannot carry out the seven life processes on its own.** C) It has too many legs. D) It can only live underwater.
- MCQ 4. How do viruses multiply or spread? A) By eating food and growing larger B) By drinking water C) **By invading a host cell and forcing it to make copies of the virus** D) By sharing seeds in the wind
- Q5 Complete the sentences about viruses. Word list: big, cells, move, protein, replicate, small. → Viruses are very **small**. Viruses are not made of **cells**. Viruses can only **replicate** when they are inside a living cell.
- Q7(c) A virus makes the mountain zebra become ill. Mia thinks a virus is a living organism. Tick to show if Mia is correct (Yes/No). Explain. → **No**. A virus is not a living organism because it **cannot reproduce on its own**.

## Lesson 3 · What is a species? Why we classify (Unit 4) → `lessons/l03-species.html`

- MCQ 5. Which term describes a group of living organisms that can mate with each other to produce fertile offspring? A) Family B) Kingdom C) **Species** D) Habitat
- MCQ 15. Why do scientists put living things into groups (classification)? A) **To make it easier to study and understand the huge variety of life on Earth** B) To keep them neatly organized in boxes forever C) To change them into different animals D) To count how many rocks are in the ground
- Q3. Explain why biologists say that lions and tigers belong to different species, even though they can sometimes reproduce together. → Organisms belong to the same species only if they reproduce together to produce **fertile** offspring. Lions and tigers produce **infertile** offspring (ligers) that cannot reproduce.
- Q4. Complete: a) Organisms that belong to the same species usually look **similar** to one another. b) They look **different** from organisms belonging to other species.
- Q7(a). The mountain zebra belongs to the species *Equus zebra*. Define the term species. → A group of living things that can produce **fertile offspring**.

## Lesson 14 · Conductors & insulators (Unit 9) → `lessons/l14-conductors-insulators.html`

- MCQ 10. What is a material called that allows electricity to flow through it easily? A) **Conductor** B) Insulator C) Absorber D) Reflector
- MCQ 11. Which of the following materials is a good electrical conductor? A) Plastic B) **Copper** C) Glass D) Rubber
- MCQ 12. What is a material called that does NOT allow electric current to pass through it easily? A) Conductor B) **Insulator** C) Circuit D) Magnet
- MCQ 13. Why are electrical wires usually coated in plastic? A) Plastic is a good conductor that speeds up electricity B) **Plastic is a good insulator that prevents electric shocks** C) Plastic makes the wire heavier D) Plastic makes the electricity shine brighter
- Short answer 6. Give two examples of electrical conductors and two of insulators. → Conductors: **steel and aluminium**. Insulators: **plastic and wood**.
- Short answer 7. Why are wires for home use not made from silver, even though silver is a better conductor than copper? → **Silver is too expensive.**
- Short answer 8. Explain why the bodies of plugs are made from plastic. → Plastic is an **insulator** that helps **prevent electric shocks**.
- Short answer 9. Explain why the pins on plugs are made from metal. → Metal is a **conductor**, so electricity can flow into the appliance.
- Short answer 10. Explain the difference between conductors and insulators in terms of particles. → Conductors have **free electrons** that can move, while insulators do **not** have free electrons that can move easily.
- Q15. Which of these materials is a good conductor of electricity? Underline one: wood, plastic, **metal**, air.
- Q16. Which of these materials could NOT be used to make electric wires? Underline one: steel, copper, **cotton**, aluminium.
